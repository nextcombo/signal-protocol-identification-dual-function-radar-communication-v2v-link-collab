Abstract: With the widespread deployment of connected autonomous vehicles (CAVs) equipped with multi-radar transceivers, there is an increasing demand for efficient environmental perception and reliable data sharing to ensure safe driving. However, the collaborative perception in the car scenario faces two major challenges: balancing performance between perception and communication, and effective resource scheduling in dynamic environments. This paper proposes a fully distributed multi-agent reinforcement learning framework based on integrated perception and communication (ISAC) technology to optimize collaborative perception performance. Firstly, the beamforming and power allocation problem is modeled as a Markov decision process (MDP); subsequently, beacon signals are designed to implement data broadcasting, and this problem is reconstructed into a more generalized multi-agent partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP). In this framework, the dual-function radar-communication (DFRC) waveform can achieve both perception and V2V data exchange without a central node, reducing interference and requiring minimal extra communication resources. Numerical results show that this method can achieve real-time perception, providing a fair and consistent quality of service (QoS) for all CAVs, and only requires a small amount of environmental prior knowledge to achieve low information age (AoI).

Introduction: In recent years, thanks to the development of key technologies such as millimeter-wave vehicle radar and vehicle communication, connected autonomous vehicles (CAVs) have achieved rapid growth. Vehicle radars can provide local perception information, aiding target detection and estimation, but other vehicles' occlusions can lead to severe fading, resulting in performance degradation. In this case, vehicle communication can support data sharing among CAVs, providing global information beyond their own perception range, effectively compensating for perception blind spots.
Collaborative perception has been widely studied in the automotive context, particularly for predicting and estimating the motion of vehicles and vulnerable road users (VRUs). Another important research direction involves controlling channel load while ensuring required performance—since the exchange of raw or processed sensor data significantly increases throughput, not only does it degrade the performance of vehicle-to-everything (V2X) links but also affects the effectiveness of collaborative perception. Existing studies have mitigated network overload and congestion through value prediction, additional schemes, and priority assessments. The European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) has also released a collective perception service (CPS) technical report, specifying the frequency and content of collective perception messages generated by CAVs. However, these efforts reduce overhead probability but may still impact communication packets. Traditional collaborative perception solutions typically rely on cellular vehicles to communicate with each other via cellular-vehicle-to-everything (C-V2X) or dedicated short-range communications (DSRC) links, requiring additional allocation of communication resources.
Integrated Sensor and Communications (ISAC) is considered a potential solution to address the above challenges. Existing research has leveraged dual-function radar-communication (DFRC) waveforms to alleviate beam tracking overhead for vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) links, designed dynamic time division multiple access (TDMA) frame structures to solve the issue of raw perception data sharing among CAVs, and built joint radar and communications (JRC) frameworks to mitigate interference between onboard radars based on frequency modulation continuous wave (FMCW). However, none of these studies have applied DFRC waveforms to collaborative perception. Additionally, existing research has deployed deep reinforcement learning (DRL) in automotive scenarios, such as optimizing the communication and perception performance of a time-division JRC system using multi-agent DRL, jointly optimizing the transmission mode selection and resource allocation for C-V2X based on federated DRL, demonstrating the scalability and robustness of DRL in dynamic automotive networks.
Inspired by the aforementioned research, this paper designs the DRL framework and integrates 77GHz ISAC technology to empower traditional V2V links. It achieves joint maximization of target perception rate without relying on existing V2X resources or central nodes, while minimizing the information age (AoI) per vehicle. To our knowledge, this paper is the first to introduce the DFRC waveform into collaborative perception, considering joint optimization of power and beam domain.
IV. Method Overview (I) System Model Considers urban road scenarios, with M CAVs randomly distributed on the roads and N vulnerable road users (VRUs) randomly distributed on sidewalks and motorways. All CAVs are equipped with independent neural networks and sufficient computing capabilities, time is divided into slots of 0.1ms, and clock synchronization is achieved through GPS.
1  
Vehicle Model: CAVs equipped with multiple radars, employing DFRC waveforms for perception and communication, replacing traditional V2V links and broadcasting beacon signals to support collaborative perception. CAVs maintain a table of perception information status, with beacon signals containing their own ID, position, and status table (including target ID, position, and AoI). At the start of each slot, CAVs update their position information and add all items in the status table to the AoI, after which they broadcast beacon signals. They then update the status table directly or obtain real-time information from other beacon signals (with the AoI reset to 0), only combining non-real-time information that is not included or has a lower AoI.
2  
Signal Transmission Model: Focuses on the direct-line-of-sight (LoS) DFRC link in the 77GHz frequency band, ignoring non-line-of-sight (NLoS) paths and certain types of interference, only considering noise-like interference. The Friis transmission equation is used to estimate beamforming gain and channel matrix gain, defining the round-trip power (assessing the received power of the DFRC link from the target reflection) and unidirectional power (assessing the interference power or communication power), and derives the perceptual signal-to-noise ratio (SINR) and communication signal-to-noise ratio formulas separately. Both are required to exceed a threshold to achieve effective perception and communication.
(II) Problem Modeling and Distributed DRL Algorithms
2  
Distributed DRL Algorithm: Reconstruct the problem into a Distributed Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and utilize the DFRC link for unified detection and information exchange processes, coordinating the convergence direction of all agents. Design independent observation, action, and reward spaces for each CAV: observational information comes from state tables and historical power, beam direction, and average interference noise ratio; actions include the indices of the beam direction codebook and power codebook; rewards consider both the number of target perceptions and AoI, balancing real-time perception performance with low latency requirements.
Conclusion: This paper proposes a novel distributed multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (DRL) scheme based on DFRC links for collaborative perception assistance. The proposed scheme enables CAVs to achieve perception and information exchange simultaneously without additional communication resources or central nodes. Numerical evaluations show that the proposed algorithm converges quickly in mobile scenarios, exhibits excellent performance, and uniformly provides consistent quality of service (QoS) to all CAVs.
